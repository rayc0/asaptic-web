§05

How the score works

Three scored signals, one hard filter, no black box.

50
C · category
Category fit against the shared taxonomy. All-or-nothing: 50 or 0, with no floor for adjacent or uncategorised notices.
30
K · keywords
Taxonomy-term overlap in the title, banded 8 / 14 / 20 by the number of distinct terms matched, plus 10 if any such term also appears in the demand text.
20
R · runway
20 at four weeks or more to closing, 10 between two and four weeks, 0 under two weeks. Absent closing dates earn nothing.
filter
market
Not scored at all. Market is a hard filter and the default view is the profile's home market — this exhibit is the Hong Kong view.

Threshold to appear on this register: 55. No single component clears it. Category fit alone contributes 50 — below the line. Keywords alone top out at 30; runway alone at 20. Category is therefore necessary but never sufficient: every row above needed corroboration from at least one other signal, and a bare category hit does not put a tender in front of anyone.

Missing data earns nothing. A notice with no closing date scores zero for runway rather than a consolation credit, so an incomplete record can never out-rank a complete one.

The score measures relevance to a supplier profile — it is not a probability of winning, and it makes no claim about price, capacity or eligibility. Those are the layers above it.

This scoring layer is deliberately simple, and published in full.
