Robot sourcing guide

Landed cost of sourcing robot components from Shenzhen: a worked example

For a single AJM-Mid joint module rated at 50 Nm and priced from US$1,099 EXW Shenzhen, Asaptic's first-pass landed-cost estimate rounds the module subtotal to US$1,100–1,250, adds estimated air freight of US$40 per module plus a US$60 base, then applies the destination-market duty band. Using the current configurator bands, that gives an indicative landed range of about US$1,200–1,400 for EU/UK, US$1,550–2,000 for the United States, and US$1,200–1,400 for APAC. The US band is a 2026 indicative tariff assumption and is especially volatile; actual duty depends on HS classification and current trade measures.

What EXW Shenzhen pricing means (and doesn't include)

EXW is a useful starting point because it tells you the supplier-side component price before international movement. It is not the number your finance team should treat as the cost of having the part in your building. For robot components, the gap between EXW and landed cost can include export handling, international freight, insurance, import duty, customs clearance, local tax handling, final delivery, documentation work, and time spent resolving classification questions.

This distinction matters most when comparing suppliers across countries or comparing a module build with a finished imported assembly. A lower EXW price can lose its advantage if freight, duty, testing, documentation, or rework are higher. A higher EXW price can still be competitive if the component arrives with clearer paperwork, better test data, and lower integration risk.

Asaptic's configurator therefore presents landed-cost bands, not just module prices. The bands are first-pass estimates for budgeting and enquiry triage. A firm quotation still has to confirm the exact product configuration, quantity, shipping route, documentation package, and destination-market classification.

Duty + freight, worked example

Use one AJM-Mid module as the example. The module is rated at 50 Nm and priced from US$1,099. The configurator rounds the EXW subtotal to the nearest US$50 for a low band, then applies a 15% high-band allowance before rounding again. For one module, that means a subtotalLow of US$1,100 and a subtotalHigh of US$1,250. Freight is US$40 per module plus a US$60 base, so freight is US$100.

Destination market Duty band used Subtotal band Freight estimate Indicative landed cost
EU / UK 0–4.5% duty, VAT excluded and typically recoverable for businesses US$1,100–1,250 US$100 US$1,200–1,400
United States 30–50%, 2026 indicative and tariff-sensitive US$1,100–1,250 US$100 US$1,550–2,000
Asia-Pacific 0–5% US$1,100–1,250 US$100 US$1,200–1,400

The exact calculation is: round the module subtotal to get the low band; round the module subtotal multiplied by 1.15 to get the high band; add duty to each band; add freight; then round the result to the nearest US$50. For the US low case, US$1,100 multiplied by 1.30 plus US$100 becomes US$1,530, which rounds to US$1,550. For the US high case, US$1,250 multiplied by 1.50 plus US$100 becomes US$1,975, which rounds to US$2,000.

Why landed cost varies by market and can change

Landed cost changes because duty rates are not universal product facts. They depend on destination market, HS classification, product description, declared use, materials, and trade measures in force at the time of import. A joint module, a motor, a reducer, a controller, and a finished robot assembly may not be treated the same way. That is why the same EXW module price can land differently in the EU/UK, United States, and APAC.

The United States needs special care in 2026 because robot-component tariff treatment can move with policy changes and classification decisions. The 30–50% band used here is deliberately time-bound and indicative. It is a budgeting assumption, not a fixed customs ruling. Quotes should be refreshed when the shipping date, product configuration, or tariff environment changes.

Freight can also move. The configurator uses US$40 per joint module plus a US$60 base as a simple first-pass air-freight estimate. That is good enough for comparing configurations, but not a substitute for shipment-level pricing after the quantity, package size, Incoterms, destination, and service level are known.

Quick answers
What does EXW mean for robot parts sourcing?
EXW is the supplier-side component price before international freight, import duty, local tax handling, customs clearance, and final delivery. It is not the landed cost.
How much duty applies to robot actuators imported to the EU, UK, US, or APAC?
The current configurator bands are EU/UK 0–4.5% excluding VAT, APAC 0–5%, and US 30–50% as a 2026 indicative band. Actual duty depends on HS classification and tariff changes.
How does Asaptic estimate landed cost for robot joint modules?
It rounds the module subtotal to a low and high band, applies the destination duty range, adds estimated freight of US$40 per module plus US$60 base, then rounds to the nearest US$50.
