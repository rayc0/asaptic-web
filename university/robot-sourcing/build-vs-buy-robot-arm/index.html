Build vs Buy: Robot Arm Cost Comparison

In one paragraph: joint-module prices make building look cheaper than buying a complete arm — until you add sizing, structure, assembly, QA-test, documentation, freight, duty and warranty. For one-off and small-batch buyers the configured build is usually cheaper like-for-like; building from parts wins when engineering, assembly and support already exist in-house. The way to decide is a like-for-like quotation on fixed payload, reach, duty cycle and destination — never a module price against a turnkey price.

What each path actually includes

Buy the modules means paying the indicative from-prices (the configurator shows US$699 / 1,099 / 1,599 across the three joint-module tiers) plus options at market-band midpoints. Everything else — torque sizing against your payload and reach, arm structure and cable routing, controller and gripper integration, assembly labour, QA-test, user manual, per-module freight (indicative US$40 per module + US$60 base), import duty by HS classification, and any warranty return freight — sits with you.

Buy the configured build means one quotation covering sized modules, assembly, QA-test with test data, preliminary manual, and a verified landed-cost breakdown for your destination market (the checkout tool estimates EU duty ≈ 0–4.5%, US ≈ 30–50% trade measures buyer-paid, APAC ≈ 0–5% — first-pass indications, confirmed at quotation). Compare the two paths only after listing which of those lines each price contains.

When each path wins

Build from modules wins when you run a robot fleet or product line: engineering amortises across units, spares stay common, and every revision stays under your control. Configured build wins for one-off cells, pilot lines and research rigs, where a single mis-sized joint or a failed import classification costs more than the integration margin. Either way, fix payload, reach, duty cycle and destination market before asking for prices — a quotation that omits those assumptions is not comparable to one that states them.

Quick answers
Is it cheaper to build a robot arm from joint modules or buy a complete arm?
Module prices alone usually favour building, but the complete-arm price bundles sizing, assembly, QA-test, documentation, warranty handling and landed cost. Like-for-like, a configured build is typically cheaper for one-off and small-batch buyers; building wins when engineering and support already exist in-house.
What hidden costs sit outside the module price?
Torque sizing and reselection risk, structure and harnessing, controller integration, QA-test time, documentation, per-module freight, import duty by HS classification, and warranty returns. Any quotation omitting these is not comparable to one that includes them.
How do I get a like-for-like quotation?
Fix payload, reach, duty cycle and destination market first, then ask every supplier for the same five lines: equipment price EXW, assembly and test scope, documentation delivered, landed-cost breakdown, and warranty terms.
