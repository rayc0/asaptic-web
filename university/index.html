Trade University — 跨境贸易大学

Free courses on cross-border trade and hardware sourcing, written for buyers and founders, not lawyers. Three tracks: the trade mechanics every importer needs, the cost benchmarks every robot buyer needs, and the sector-specific playbooks for Asaptic's advanced sourcing verticals.

In one paragraph: Asaptic Trade University is a free, growing lesson library covering three tracks — Core Trade (Incoterms, landed cost, customs documentation, payment terms — the mechanics of any cross-border deal), Robot Sourcing (what components actually cost, AMR vs. AGV and other buy-decision comparisons, supplier vetting), and Advanced Verticals (sector playbooks for BESS, medical devices, EV chargers, and photonics sourcing). Lessons lead with a direct answer, back it with a comparison table where the question is numeric, and close with a short FAQ. No registration required.

Three tracks

Track 1 · Core Trade

The mechanics every first-time importer or exporter needs: Incoterms, landed-cost math, customs documentation, payment terms, and how a cross-border deal actually closes. Start here if you have never imported anything before.

Track 2 · Robot Sourcing

What robot components and platforms actually cost, how to compare buy options (AMR vs. AGV, build vs. buy a joint module), and how to vet a supplier before you wire a deposit. Commercial companion to the engineering-focused Robot 101.

Track 3 · Advanced Verticals

Sector-specific sourcing playbooks for Asaptic's advanced verticals — BESS and solar inverters, medical and clinical devices, EV-charger power modules, and deep-tech photonics — where compliance and qualification add real cost and lead time.

Frequently asked questions
What is Asaptic Trade University?
A free, growing lesson library covering three tracks: Core Trade (Incoterms, landed cost, customs, payment), Robot Sourcing (component cost benchmarks, buy-decision comparisons, supplier vetting), and Advanced Verticals (sector playbooks for BESS, medical devices, EV chargers, and photonics). No registration required.
Who is Trade University written for?
First-time importers, procurement teams, and hardware founders who need working knowledge of trade mechanics and sourcing economics — not a law degree. Lessons favor plain language, worked numbers, and comparison tables over legal citation.
Is this the same as Robot 101?
No. Robot 101 is an engineering primer on how robots are built. The Robot Sourcing track here is commercial — cost benchmarks, buy vs. build, and comparison shopping for the same components. The two link to each other where relevant.
How often are new lessons added?
Trade University is an active content hub — new lessons are published on a rolling basis across all three tracks. Lessons marked "coming soon" are on the near-term roadmap.
